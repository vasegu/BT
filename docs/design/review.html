M05 + M06 · CUSTOMER MOMENTS
The first week. The quiet router.
Mark uses familiar moments to explain why a generic response misses the customer’s situation.
We compare three households after the same event, then change the shared operational context.


M03 + M07 · RESTRAINT AND OWNERSHIP
Sometimes help means staying quiet.
Routine friction should disappear. An unresolved problem still needs a human who owns it.
Maya’s notification is held. Daniel’s case retains Aisha and the 21:15 promise.


M09 + M10 · MEMORY AND JUDGEMENT
What we know changes what we do.
Customer memory must be useful at the moment of decision, with reasoning that can be checked.
Evidence lanes, a separate operational state and typed domain questions feed an explicit arbiter decision.


M11 + M13 · GOVERNED ACTION
A recommendation must survive reality.
Mark puts the safe route to action on equal footing with the intelligence.
Permission and capacity are checked at commitment; failed or rejected actions retain receipts.


M12 · OUTCOME LEARNING
Follow-through creates the next memory.
The loop should establish whether the customer’s situation actually improved.
Delivery, technical restoration, customer confirmation and paid adoption remain distinct observations.


OUR ADDITION · FROM SAINSBURY’S AND SOHO
Give the operation a memory, too.
Incidents, available capacity and typical demand constrain what can usefully happen next.
Operational records and fitted forecasts meet customer history before an action is selected.
