# Mark’s feedback — acceptance and presenter route

30 September 2026. Implementation follows `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-30-marks-feedback-story-value.md`.

## What changed

The presenter heading follows the selected household’s outcome, rather than forcing a shared headline onto three different experiences. The arbiter now joins cited customer, operational and authority evidence in one compact source-linked strip, shared with the memory inspector. Missing authority evidence is named explicitly. The projection respects occurrence, receipt, revision and decision time; outside-scope shared incident records cannot displace the household’s own telemetry.

Household detail uses the same component in overview and inspector. The overview keeps members and device counts by type; the inspector retains individual devices and dated history. Semantic-memory charts explain similarity, retrieval and the separate role of eligibility checks. Profile notes describe contextual relevance rather than claiming every attribute directly drives implemented policy.

Outcome headings no longer infer closure, product adoption or message delivery merely from reaching a later beat. Maya’s recorded quiet repair remains visible in later moments. Outcome evidence distinguishes observation from causation. Future product quotes and agent mandates remain explicitly illustrative.

Agent review starts with two actual paired contexts: an unverified gamer claim and a changed incident-scope input. Both open the underlying input and resulting action. The map is a global static policy study, not a live-model safety score. Tone is not measured by that artifact. The relevant comparison uses an existing incident probe; no new usage-factor experiment or fabricated outlier was added.

## Presenter routes

Each story can stand alone. Use the same clock; select the household before beginning. Stay on the relevant section while advancing; use changed markers to choose a deliberate digression.

### Daniel — remember, keep the promise, verify

1. 20:45 / Customer memory: proactive case, failed restart, named adviser and promised callback. Open memory for relationship/history if asked.
2. 21:00 / Arbiter: why a generic restart or redundant message is inappropriate. Inspect the remembered promise and actual candidate checks.
3. 21:03–21:12 / Operational memory: incident scope and dated restoration evidence; the conversational agent did not physically repair the network.
4. 21:15–21:18 / Actions and phone: callback kept and customer confirmation. Confirmation is distinct from formal case closure.
5. Monday / Actions: monitoring still has its own deadline. Six weeks / outcome evidence: show recorded completion and closure.

### Sam — value from everything purchased

1. 20:45 / Customer memory: new household, products purchased, delivery versus first use.
2. 21:00–21:18 / Arbiter and phone: setup intersects a scoped incident; then verify first use rather than infer it from delivery.
3. Saturday–Monday / Customer memory and phone: included services still unused, then guidance and recorded engagement.
4. Six weeks / Arbiter: actual usage, consent and approval support a relevant offer. No claim of purchase or incremental revenue without acceptance evidence.
5. Optional / Actions → What we learned: illustrated onboarding comparison; association is not measured causal lift.

### Maya — act when needed, communicate appropriately

1. 20:45 / Customer memory: recorded rhythm and stated contact preferences.
2. 21:00–21:12 / Arbiter: observation and fresh service evidence take precedence over simply trusting a habit.
3. Saturday / Operational memory and phone: the actual overnight intervention and the morning explanation.
4. Monday / Actions: inspect the observation window and outcome records.
5. Six weeks / Customer memory: new devices and relationship context do not override missing marketing consent. Optional future vision shows a customer-initiated delegated request.
